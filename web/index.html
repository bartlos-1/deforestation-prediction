What this is

An atlas of observed tree-cover loss and a model of where loss is likely in the next 1–3 years. It began as a DATA 501 capstone at the University of Calgary by Arij Ashar and Yurii Bezborodov, and was rebuilt in 2026.

How the model works

  • Target: for every pixel that is still forest at the end of year T, does Hansen record loss in T+1…T+h?
  • Features (all known by the end of T): Landsat growing-season reflectance and 1- and 3-year changes, terrain, canopy cover, distance to and density of recent loss, recent wildfire (MODIS), and roads, protected areas and settlements where available.
  • Validation: trained on earlier years and tested on later years, in ~15 km blocks the model never saw. The scores are weighted back to the full forest area.

Read the map with care

  • Loss is not the same as deforestation. Hansen loss includes wildfire and harvest followed by regrowth. In this region most of it is not a permanent change in land use.
  • Wildfire is hard to predict. Its timing depends on weather the model doesn’t see. The Models tab reports fire and other loss separately.
  • Cells are aggregates. Each square is a 0.04° cell (~3–4 km).
